01. Overview

Learning through recreation

I recreated Mirror.report's landing page in Penpot to sharpen my observation and visual execution. Its generous whitespace, contrasting type styles and shifts between light and dark sections made it a useful study in composition.

I used the original page as a reference, focusing on its proportions, alignment and section flow.

02. Recreation approach

Understanding the details

01

Typography

I recreated the contrasting type styles, studying how scale, line breaks and text width establish hierarchy.

02

Layout and rhythm

I matched whitespace and alternating image-text sections, paying attention to the proportions that connect each part of the page.

03

Colour and imagery

I reproduced the light-to-dark transitions, image crops and rounded frames to retain the reference's visual balance.

04

Fine details

Logo rows, subtle dividers and small navigation elements challenged me to maintain precision beyond the main sections.

03. Design considerations

Working with a static format

The reference included animated and scroll-based elements. My recreation focused on its still appearance, retaining the section order and visual relationships within a static page.

This meant looking closely at how text placement, contrast and image composition carry emphasis when movement is absent. I kept those adaptations close to the original visual direction.

04. Reflection

What I took away

This exercise taught me to look at relationships between elements more carefully. A heading's width changes its line breaks, and a small spacing adjustment changes how nearby content feels grouped.

Working from a reference also gave me a clearer way to judge my execution. I learned to look beyond whether an individual element seemed correct and consider how it affected the balance of the whole section.

My main takeaway was a more deliberate eye for detail: noticing the small decisions that make a page feel consistent.
