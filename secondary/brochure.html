Crimson Flame Restaurant

A tri-fold inspired digital brochure concept, highlighting restaurant story, signature dishes, and brand personality.

Role
Visual Designer
Platform
Digital
Tools
Inkscape

01. Overview

Introducing the restaurant through its food

I wanted Crimson Flame to feel bold and welcoming, with the food taking the lead. The concept brings the restaurant's introduction, story and menu together in a brochure-style presentation.

I began with an existing restaurant brochure as a composition reference, then adapted the identity, wording and layout for Crimson Flame. My focus was on visual presentation and menu organisation in Inkscape.

02. Design decisions

Connecting personality with clarity

01

A bold focal point

I used crimson red to represent the flame colour, and give the brand story a strong presence, carrying the same colour into buttons to connect the panels.

02

Food-led discovery

Large food photographs introduce the variety of dishes, while short category labels give each image a clear purpose within the menu.

03

Type with distinct roles

Serif headings bring an editorial feel, while simpler body text separates the restaurant's story from practical menu information.

04

A clear next step

Prominent menu buttons connect the introductory content with further exploration, making the intended next action easy to identify.

03. Visual direction

Giving each panel its own role

White panels provide space for introductions and menu content, while the red About Us panel creates contrast. Rounded image tiles and repeated colour accents keep the different compositions visually connected.

The folded mockup brings the panels together in one view, alongside a phone frame showing the menu categories. It presents the relationship between the restaurant's identity and its digital menu concept.

04. Reflection

What I took away

This project helped me think more carefully about what deserves attention within a limited space. Balancing food photography with the story and menu text made hierarchy central to the layout.

I also learned how repeated colours, type styles and image treatments can connect panels with different compositions. Working from a reference helped me practise adapting a layout to new content while keeping the presentation coherent.
