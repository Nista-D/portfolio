Furniture Ecommerce Landing Page Concept

Furniche.

A furniture landing page concept exploring how lifestyle imagery, room-based browsing and visual storytelling can make a collection feel connected to everyday life.

Role
UI Designer
Platform
Web
Tools
Figma
View Figma prototype

01. Overview

Making furniture feel personal

I wanted Furniche to feel warm and premium, with a focus on the moments furniture makes possible: a slow morning, a shared meal or a comfortable place to work.

For this fictional brand, I explored how content, imagery and section flow could guide visitors from imagining their spaces to discovering individual products.

02. Design decisions

From atmosphere to exploration

01

A sense of home

I led with interior photography to establish warmth and help visitors imagine a space before exploring individual products.

02

Browse by room

I grouped furniture around familiar spaces, using everyday stories to connect each category with how people live.

03

Discover through interaction

Product cards flip on hover to reveal details and a “Discover More” action, keeping the initial view focused on imagery.

04

Confidence to explore

Testimonials and service promises introduce reassurance, followed by options to continue browsing or seek design guidance.

03. Visual direction

Warmth through restraint

A warm grey-beige background and brown accents connect the page with the tones of the furniture photography. White cards separate content, while rounded corners soften the presentation.

Brown emphasis within headings and CTAs create a recurring visual cue. Generous spacing gives the imagery room and separates the different stages of the page.

04. Reflection

What I took away

Furniche taught me to think about content and layout together. Writing around everyday moments helped me decide what to emphasise and how to connect the sections into a coherent experience.

I also learned that consistency doesn't require every section to follow the same layout. A shared palette, typography and spacing can hold different compositions together while giving each section its own purpose.

The biggest takeaway was learning to connect visual choices to an intention: what I wanted someone to notice, feel or explore next.
